The secret language of DNA.

Imagine a tiny instruction book inside almost every cell of your body. That book is written using just four chemical letters.

Meet the four letters

DNA stands for Deoxyribonucleic Acid. Its information is encoded using four nitrogenous bases:

A — Adenine

T — Thymine

C — Cytosine

G — Guanine

The pairing rule

In ordinary double-stranded DNA, adenine pairs with thymine, while cytosine pairs with guanine.

Think of them as molecular partners that help hold the DNA double helix together.

Why does this matter?

DNA stores genetic information. Scientists study DNA sequences to investigate inherited traits, genetic variation, diseases, and evolutionary relationships.

Bioinformatics helps us analyze these sequences using algorithms and software.
